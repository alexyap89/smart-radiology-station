3D + 4D · the lab

A photorealistic model, from the scan she already had.

Cinematic volume rendering: bone, soft tissue and the way they relate, in one image. No second acquisition, no additional dose.

Charcot arthropathy. The talar dome is articulating with the midfoot instead of the tibia, and a large effusion has displaced the ankle tendons.

Tier 0 is the default, and printing is arriving.

The cheapest modality that answers the clinical question wins, so most complex fractures stop at digital reconstruction. A print has to earn its place — it exists because handling the fracture changes the plan or the conversation. The models on the table are the printed end of that spectrum.

Live clinical service. Reconstruction S$82–S$299; printed models S$560–S$1,020 depending on tier. 279 complex-fracture candidates in about two years; 50–65 prints planned this year against a 100-unit target, and each print not made avoids 0.73–1.33 kWh and 0.6–1.3 kg CO₂.

3D + 4D · less dose

The image got quieter, so the scan could get smaller.

3D + 4D · the joint in motion

A static scan shows the joint. This shows what it does.

The detector covers 160 mm in one rotation, so the same joint can be acquired repeatedly through a coached arc of motion and reconstructed into phases. For a wrist that clicks, an elbow that locks or a shoulder that dislocates, the diagnosis is the thing static imaging cannot show.

Every figure above is a pre-committed target written before the first case. 4D is an adjunct dataset: it does not replace MRI or fluoroscopy, and it does not change who reports the scan.

Why it needs the lab.

4D CT without post-processing is a cine loop. Acquisition, reconstruction and 3D visualisation are one pipeline, so they are one proof.

The real constraint is people.

Patient coaching runs at one and a half to two times a standard CT, and the motion analysis lands on the 3D Lab. The limit is preparation time and post-processing skill, not the scanner.

Takeaway

Four programmes, and exactly what each one can prove.

01 · RbFracture · validated

The fracture copilot for emergency radiology. In 1,102 consecutive radiographs it caught 6 fractures the first reports missed; radiologists caught the 7 it missed. 96.6% sensitivity, 99.2% negative predictive value.

02 · 3D + 4D · service and pilot

Cinematic rendering, and printing, from the scan already acquired — digital-first, so most cases never reach a printer. The 4D pilot is building a diagnosis static imaging cannot make: labelled a pilot, no outcome data yet.

03 · MRI acceleration · initial evaluation

AI reconstruction shortened MRI scans by about 37% across 8 musculoskeletal anatomies. Less time inside the bore is the patient-facing half: less to endure, less to abandon, less to repeat.

04 · pRIMe · validated in-house

A preparation experience a clinician wrote: the room, the breath-hold and the sound, in the patient's own language. 16 of 16 patients rated it positively at one month and would recommend it.

What each one rests on

  1. RbFracture — a blinded audit of 1,102 consecutive studies; manuscript in preparation.
  2. 3D + 4D — a live service with its own cost and carbon data; the 4D arm is a pre-committed pilot protocol.
  3. MRI acceleration — an initial evaluation, musculoskeletal only.
  4. pRIMe — one department, 16 patients, one month.

What we do not claim

  1. No published outcome language: the manuscript is not published.
  2. No achieved dose saving on CT — the ladder states targets, tier by tier.
  3. No 4D result of any kind yet; the figures are the protocol's gates.
  4. No theatre-time or cost saving for the 3D Lab until it is measured.

What this does not claim. Studies negative on both AI and the report were not independently re-read, so sensitivity is likely an overestimate. The 37% applies to musculoskeletal protocols, not to every scan. The dose ladder and the 4D pilot are labelled targets, not measured outcomes. The manuscript is in preparation, not published.

The pattern: measure the problem, validate the tool on our own patients, keep the radiologist as the final interpreter, and let the people who use the tools build them. That is what makes it a model other hospitals could adopt.

Smart Radiology · summary

What changed, and what it rests on

  1. Validate before deployment. 1,102 consecutive ED radiographs were audited with the AI output withheld from the reporting radiologist.
  2. Keep the radiologist in the decision. The tool flags; the radiologist interprets. AI caught 6 fractures the initial reports missed; radiologists caught 7 the AI missed.
  3. Answer the patient's question. Dual-energy CT settled an occult fracture the same night, and a 360° preparation experience got an anxious patient through her first MRI.
