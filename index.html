3D + 4D · denoising

Turn the denoiser up, and the dose comes down.

A denoiser rebuilds the image from the same raw data, and its strength is a setting — so a scan can be acquired at lower dose and still read. That dose pays for new dimensions of CT, like a joint imaged repeatedly in motion.

One scan, three reconstructions: nothing extra was taken. A dose target with a quality floor, reported tier by tier — not a saving we have banked.

Takeaway

Four programmes, and what each one can prove.

01 · RbFracture · validated

A fracture copilot in the emergency department. 1,102 consecutive radiographs; 6 fractures caught that the first reports missed.

02 · 3D + 4D · service and pilot

Photorealistic models from the scan already taken, digital-first so most cases never reach a printer. The 4D pilot is still a pilot.

03 · MRI acceleration · evaluation

About 37% shorter across 8 musculoskeletal anatomies. Less time in the bore is the part the patient feels.

04 · pRIMe · in-house

The scanner rehearsed at home, in the patient's own language. 16 of 16 positive at one month.

What this does not claim. Studies negative on both AI and the report were not re-read, so sensitivity is likely an overestimate. The 37% is musculoskeletal only. The dose ladder and the 4D pilot are targets, not results. The manuscript is in preparation.

The pattern: measure the problem, validate on our own patients, keep the radiologist as final interpreter, and let the people who use the tools build them.

Smart Radiology · summary

What changed, and what it rests on

  1. Validate before deployment. 1,102 consecutive ED radiographs were audited with the AI output withheld from the reporting radiologist.
  2. Keep the radiologist in the decision. The tool flags; the radiologist interprets. AI caught 6 fractures the initial reports missed; radiologists caught 7 the AI missed.
  3. Answer the patient's question. Dual-energy CT settled an occult fracture the same night, and a 360° preparation experience got an anxious patient through her first MRI.

In one line: four programmes, each labelled for what it can currently prove — a validated fracture copilot, a live 3D and 4D lab, an initial evaluation on MRI acceleration, and a clinician-written preparation tool.
